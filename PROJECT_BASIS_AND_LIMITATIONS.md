# Project Basis, Assumptions and Limitations

## 1. Project identity

- **Project:** Skyline Residency – Tower A (hypothetical academic case study)
- **Study location:** Sector 150, Noida, Delhi-NCR (assumed)
- **Configuration:** 2 basements + Ground + 24 typical floors + terrace
- **Apartments:** 96
- **Height above ground:** 79.5 m
- **Built-up area:** 27,630 m² (approximately 297,407 sq ft)
- **Structural concept assumed for planning:** RCC core/shear walls with flat slabs and perimeter beams; M30–M40 concrete and Fe 550D reinforcement
- **Foundation concept assumed for planning:** raft at approximately 9 m depth, contiguous-pile shoring and basement waterproofing
- **Contract period in the model:** 1 June 2025 to 30 November 2027 (30 months)
- **Monitoring status date:** 31 October 2026 (Month 17)

## 2. Academic-use statement

This is a self-initiated academic planning and project-controls case study for a hypothetical residential tower. It is not a live construction project, a Godrej Properties project, a client-approved estimate, a tender submission, an approved construction baseline or a construction-ready engineering package.

All project inputs—including building configuration, dimensions, quantities, rates, budget allocations, progress, actual costs, material movements, quality results, safety statistics, contractor bills and risk scores—are assumed or illustrative. They do not describe an actual site, contractor, vendor or company.

## 3. Basis of the planning model

The model demonstrates a planning-to-control workflow using assumed project parameters and an Excel-based linked model. The report describes a quantity take-off using assumed structural dimensions and element-level factors, a BOQ with indicative Delhi-NCR rates, a work-breakdown structure and logic-linked schedule, cost loading, Earned Value Management, material planning, steel reconciliation, QA/QC and safety registers, contractor RA-bill tracking and risk reporting.

The academic report refers to IS 456, IS 1786, IS 1200, NBC 2016 and CPWD-style rate-analysis conventions as reference context. Mention of a standard is not a certification that the hypothetical design or data has been checked or approved against that standard.

## 5. Schedule and monitoring assumptions

The report describes 28 work activities in seven groups plus one project-wide preliminaries activity (29 activities in the model), with finish-to-start and start-to-start relationships and lags. The planned typical-floor structural cycle is 12 days. The baseline schedule is modelled over 30 months.

The status date used in the report is 31 October 2026 (Month 17). The report's planned value (PV), earned value (EV), actual cost (AC), progress, SPI, CPI, forecasts, material records, steel reconciliation, concrete test records, safety indicators and contractor payment data are illustrative model inputs or outputs—not verified site records.

Reported Month 17 summary values include PV ₹43.09 crore, EV ₹40.92 crore, AC ₹42.93 crore, SPI 0.950, CPI 0.953 and CPI-based EAC ₹95.31 crore. The report also presents a schedule/cost-efficiency scenario and a forecast completion date. These values illustrate how the model calculates and interprets project-control indicators; they must not be represented as actual project performance.

## 6. Key limitations

1. No approved architectural or structural drawings, geotechnical investigation, project specifications, bar-bending schedules, vendor quotations or executed contract documents were supplied for this case study.
2. Quantities are planning-level estimates derived from assumed dimensions and element factors. They are not a drawing-based, independently checked detailed measurement or tender BOQ.
3. Rates and cost allowances are illustrative. They are not a market survey, vendor quotation, negotiated rate or approved budget.
4. Schedule dates, productivity assumptions, actual progress and cost forecasts are scenario data. They are not commitments by a contractor or developer.
5. Quality, testing, safety, procurement, steel reconciliation and RA-bill entries are examples of monitoring registers; they are not actual inspection, laboratory, safety or payment certificates.
6. The case study does not provide structural analysis or design, foundation design, geotechnical design, detailed MEP design, fire/life-safety design, statutory approvals, construction method statements or professional sign-off.
7. Any code or statutory compliance for a real project must be established using the applicable current requirements, approved project documents and competent professional review.

## 7. Appropriate use

Use this project to demonstrate academic understanding of quantity take-off, BOQ structure, construction sequencing, CPM/Gantt planning, cost loading, EVM, procurement planning, QA/QC monitoring, steel reconciliation, RA-bill tracking, safety registers, risk management and monthly reporting.

Do not use the workbook or report to procure materials, execute work, certify quantities, approve payments, design structural elements or make site-safety decisions. For a real project, replace all assumptions with approved drawings, specifications, geotechnical information, contract terms, verified site measurements, current quotations and reviewed project records, with sign-off by the responsible professionals.
